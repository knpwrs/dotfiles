# Self-check

Run this against the finished text before returning it. Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure and run the list again. Do this yourself; do not hand it to a separate evaluator agent.

## Hard bans

1. Search for the em dash character. Zero occurrences outside quoted source material.
2. No banned verbs, adjectives, transitions, openers, closers, empty phrases, or inflated symbolism from `phrases.md`.
3. Every number is real and attributable. Nothing invented, nothing estimated silently.
4. Every attributed statement or position traces to a real source. No positions inferred from someone's role or employer.
5. Every date, event, and case study is real. No narrative scenarios presented as fact.
6. No emoji in headings or bullets. No exclamation marks added for enthusiasm.
7. No research-process narration. Nothing about what could not be found, and no "as of [date]" attached to your own search failure.

## Grounding

8. Every claim ends on a concrete detail rather than an assertion of importance.
9. Intensifiers have been replaced by the facts they stood in for, or cut. Every "unusually", "surprisingly", "notably", "strikingly" style comparative either names its baseline in the same sentence or is gone.
10. Specific facts survived the edit instead of being smoothed into generic significance.
11. Hedging is under three per paragraph, and what remains marks real uncertainty rather than habit.
12. Every "experts agree" style attribution is either sourced or gone. Nothing was invented to fill a gap.
13. No sentence could sit unchanged on any other site in the category.
14. Every contrast between two things names the mechanism, part, version, or date that makes the difference real.

## Structure

15. No binary contrasts, negative listings, rhetorical setups, or throat-clearing openers.
16. No faux-insight setups, colon reveals, importance puffery, superficial `-ing` clauses, fake-strong verbs, or synonym cycling.
17. Active voice with human subjects where a human acted. No inanimate thing performing a human verb.
18. No fake-profound kicker. If one existed, it was deleted rather than rewritten into a better metaphor.
19. No summary-recap ending. The piece ends on a concrete point, takeaway, or next action.
20. Every heading names what its section contains rather than teasing it. No parenthetical clarifications in headings.
21. No three consecutive sections or paragraphs built from the same template or opening word.
22. Sentence lengths vary. Something short, something long, no stacked punchy fragments.
23. Formatting organizes rather than decorates: no decorative bold, no bullets where prose reads better, no headings over two-sentence sections, no lists padded to three.
24. Sentence case after colons unless grammar, a proper noun, a title, or code requires otherwise.

## Voice

25. The edit added no claims, examples, statistics, quotes, or opinions the writer did not have.
26. The writer's vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, profanity, uncertainty, and digressions survived.
27. Strong human sentences were left alone, including ones you would have written differently.
28. Cutting was proportional to the actual slop. Nothing was compressed for its own sake.
29. Paragraphs are not uniformly tidy. Rough edges that belong to the writer are still there.
30. Structure and detours are intact, or the reorganization is explained in **What changed**.
31. Genuinely tangled sentences are fixed. Clear spoken cadence and characteristic fragments remain.
32. The writer would recognize this as their own writing.

## Final read

33. Read it aloud. Nothing sounds unnatural said to a colleague.
34. No markup artifacts: `oaicite`, `contentReference`, `turn0search0`, `grok_card`, `attributableIndex`.
35. Nothing is reported as complete, verified, or tested when it is not.
36. The output is the full text plus a short **What changed** list, with no preamble.
37. For an audit, the response names each pattern with a quoted line and a short fix, plus the six scores, and does not rewrite the draft or claim to know what wrote it.
